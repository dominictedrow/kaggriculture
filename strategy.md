Kaggriculture: 7-Page Strategy Breakdown
Consolidated strategy framework + new findings from a ~68th-place competitor script
Objective: maximize final cash generated by every action, dollar, worker, tile, and turn.
Executive Thesis
The strongest direction is not a fully reactive RL agent and not a completely fixed script. The combined evidence supports a hierarchical hybrid: build a small library of highly optimized deterministic strategies, select among them, execute each through a strong precomputed trajectory, and let a lightweight watcher intervene only when observed conditions materially diverge from what the active strategy expects.
What the original strategy document established
Actions are the scarce resource. Movement, hiring, harvesting, planting, weed removal, and land purchases all carry opportunity cost.
The real objective is final money per action—not maximum production.
Demand matters more than raw price; production should be tied to expected profit/action.
Centralized livestock/Ring is a leading layout hypothesis; Solid, Checker, and X remain important alternatives to test.
Headstart, Melon Rush, demand-driven Wool, early capacity investment, and strategic liquidation are the major hypotheses.
RL from scratch is low priority; engineered strategies followed by learned strategy switching is much more promising.
What the competitor script adds
A strong competitor is using a largely precomputed trajectory rather than solving the entire game online.
Runtime adaptation is deliberately narrow: weed recovery and reordering already-planned sell slots.
The script models nonlinear market price impact and adds bounded Town demand pressure to sell ordering.
Important correction: the script defines REBALANCE_ACTIONS as the same action backbone as LEGACY_ACTIONS. The regime switch changes execution logic, not the underlying farm trajectory.
Because the competitor is around 68th, the script should be treated as a useful baseline and evidence—not as proof that fixed trajectories are the winning meta.
Strategic conclusion
The competitor validates the value of a strong trajectory layer. Our strategy document supplies the missing higher-level strategy library and economic reasoning. The watcher model is the control layer that can combine them without overreacting.

1. Core Economic Model
The optimization target
Kaggriculture should be treated as an action-economics problem. Every action consumed by movement, production, expansion, hiring, maintenance, or selling has a shadow price. A strategy that produces more units but requires substantially more actions can lose to a lower-output strategy with better action efficiency.

Production should be demand-aware
The document's central market hypothesis is that high price alone is not enough. A commodity becomes strategically attractive when price, demand, growth time, market behavior, and movement combine into high expected profit/action. Wool, milk, and strawberries are therefore conditional rather than permanently dominant; wheat and carrots are useful baseline insurance; melons are the major high-upside/high-downside opening candidate.

The time-horizon rule
The late game is not merely a smaller version of the early game. When remaining turns approach the combined growth + harvest + sale time of a crop, new planting becomes economically irrational. The final phase should prioritize harvesting, selling, minimizing movement, and avoiding investments that cannot recover.
Core rule: Maximize the amount of final cash generated by every available action, dollar, worker, tile, and turn.

The original document's strongest idea is to treat layouts and openings as modes rather than mutually exclusive permanent identities. Different phases and market regimes favor different policies. The system should therefore maintain several strong candidates and switch only when the expected benefit exceeds the cost of switching.

The document's strongest combined concept is an Adaptive Ring / Headstart Hybrid: aggressive opening; land and worker investment only when payback is long enough; high-value crop exploitation such as melons; then central livestock plus wheat/carrot baseline; demand-driven replacement of low-value production; and finally deliberate liquidation.
Layout philosophy
Ring is the leading general-purpose layout because it attempts to preserve crop density while keeping livestock maintenance efficient.
Solid is the strongest simple baseline because it minimizes movement complexity and provides a clean melon-to-safe-crop transition.
Checker trades density for flexibility and should win only if frequent switching is valuable enough to pay for the lost specialization.
X should remain a test candidate, not a default: accessibility must demonstrably outweigh fragmented crop production.
The true objective is not 'put livestock in the middle'; it is minimizing expected maintenance movement while preserving production density.
Opening experiments
Reverse-engineer Headstart first.
Compare Headstart, Melon Rush, HealthStone, 4-Quad, Wool/Wheat, and other top openings under identical conditions.
Record final cash plus early/mid/late cash, movement, production, land, workers, and missed opportunities—not just the final score.
Convert the best openings into reusable trajectories rather than hard-coding one winner forever.

3. What the Competitor Script Actually Teaches Us
Treat it as a strong baseline—not a final answer
The competitor's script is valuable because it shows a real submission built around offline optimization and limited runtime intervention. However, the user-provided leaderboard context places this competitor around 68th, so the correct interpretation is: this architecture is demonstrably viable, but there is likely room above it.
Backbone: precomputed trajectory
The script explicitly describes its farm backbone as a fit-only public THUNDER THUNDER trajectory selected on a later current-meta panel. At runtime it retrieves the action associated with the current step. This is fundamentally different from a policy that recomputes the best action from scratch at every state.
Runtime intervention is narrow
Mechanism
What it does
What it does not do
Weed recovery
If a planned PLANT/BUILD_PASTURE action encounters a weed, DIG is inserted and the intended action is replayed afterward.
It does not redesign the farm or choose a new crop strategy.
Sell ordering
Ranks existing SELL slots using nonlinear price impact, then bounded demand urgency.
It does not create, remove, or resize ordinary SELL orders.
Regime detection
Uses townCenterSellInterval to distinguish legacy vs rebalance execution.
It does not select two fundamentally different farm trajectories—the code aliases REBALANCE_ACTIONS to LEGACY_ACTIONS.

Market-impact modeling is the most transferable idea
The script estimates the cost of selling a quantity by comparing the current quote with the quote after adding that quantity to market inventory. Its impact score is approximately quantity × max(0, current price − later price). This captures a key economic fact: selling is not merely 'receive current price'; the sale can worsen the marginal price.
Demand is a bounded modifier
The script reconstructs demand/day from unlocked shops, their product lists, sell intervals, Town Center behavior, and day/regime effects. It then adds only a bounded demand multiplier (DEMAND_ALPHA = 0.25) to the sell-order score. This is a useful design lesson: demand can guide execution without being allowed to completely overthrow a good base trajectory.
What this means for our strategy
Do not copy the trajectory. Copy the architectural lesson: discover strong trajectories offline, then make online intervention cheap, local, and evidence-based. Our system should go one level higher by maintaining multiple trajectories and deciding when to stay, adjust, or switch.

4. The Watcher Model: The Missing Control Layer
Watcher objective
The watcher should not be a second full agent. Its job is to answer a narrower question: 'Is the active strategy still behaving like the profitable trajectory we expected?' Most turns should produce no intervention.
Three intervention levels
Level
Trigger
Allowed response
0 — Continue
Observed performance is within expected range.
Follow the trajectory exactly.
1 — Local correction
A localized deviation occurs but strategy economics remain sound.
Reorder sales, repair weeds, adjust a worker/crop allocation, or make another small correction.
2 — Strategic intervention
Persistent performance gap or regime change makes another strategy materially better after switching cost.
Return to strategy selector; switch trajectories/modes.

What the watcher should monitor
Current and recent cash/action performance.
Commodity prices versus expected trajectory and trend.
Demand/shop availability and expected absorption.
Inventory relative to market equilibrium and demand capacity.
Land, worker, livestock, and production utilization.
Remaining turns relative to crop growth + harvest + sale time.
Unexpected weeds or movement bottlenecks.
Expected performance of alternative strategies, not merely current price.
Do not let the watcher thrash
A switch should require more than a single bad observation. The watcher should use hysteresis: a competing strategy must outperform the current strategy by a meaningful margin for a sustained period, or its projected final cash must exceed the current strategy by enough to cover the cost of switching.
Switch criterion: Expected benefit of switching − switching cost > required safety margin.
Watcher as anomaly detector
The best watcher compares the observed trajectory with an expected trajectory. If a Melon Rush normally produces a rising profit/action curve and the realized curve stalls or reverses for long enough, that deviation is more meaningful than a single low melon price. This lets the system react to sustained failure rather than noise.

5. Final Hybrid Architecture
Recommended system
The combined architecture is hierarchical. Each layer has a narrower job than the one above it, which reduces computational complexity and prevents overreaction.
Layer
Responsibility
Example
Strategy library
Store tested strategic modes and trajectories.
Headstart, Melon Rush, Ring, Solid, Wool/Wheat, Liquidation.
Strategy selector
Choose the best candidate for the current regime and horizon.
Select Ring because wool demand is strong and switching cost is low.
Trajectory/policy
Execute the optimized action sequence for the selected strategy.
Follow a precomputed 300-step farm plan.
Execution layer
Handle action alignment and known local mechanics.
Route movement, synchronize workers, execute sales.
Watcher
Detect meaningful deviations and decide whether to intervene.
Current $/action is 20% below expected for 15 turns.
Local optimizer
Make minimal corrections before abandoning a strategy.
Reorder sell slots based on price impact and demand.

Control loop
Strategy selection → execute trajectory → observe state → watcher checks deviation → continue, locally correct, or switch → repeat. The key is that the watcher is above the trajectory, not constantly fighting it.
Where RL/search fits
The original document's most promising RL formulation remains: engineered strategies first, then learn when to switch between them. A small model could eventually estimate P(continue), P(local correction), and P(switch to strategy B) from recent trajectory statistics. This is a much smaller learning problem than learning the entire game from scratch.

Why this should outperform either extreme
A pure fixed script is efficient but brittle. A fully adaptive optimizer is flexible but may spend too much computation and may overreact to noise. The hybrid keeps the reliability and action efficiency of deterministic trajectories while gaining a controlled ability to exploit genuine regime changes.

6. Development Roadmap & Competitive Priorities
Build in this order
Reverse-engineer top openings and strong public trajectories. Treat the ~68th-place script as a baseline, not an oracle.
Map exact market/shop mechanics: demand caps, price changes, shop expiration, purchasing effects, and predictability.
Quantify movement cost and route efficiency. This is a top-ranked hypothesis and may matter more than small crop-selection differences.
Test Ring vs Solid vs X vs Checker with identical seeds/conditions and record movement, production, actions, and final cash.
Optimize Melon Rush entry and, especially, liquidation/exit timing.
Optimize land and worker purchase timing using break-even against remaining turns.
Build a deterministic strategy library with explicit expected trajectories and switching costs.
Implement the watcher as a conservative anomaly detector with Continue / Local Correction / Switch outputs.
Only after the deterministic system is strong, test ML/RL/search for strategy switching.

Priority hypotheses
H1: Ring beats X/Checker because production density outweighs marginal movement savings.
H2: Melon Rush becomes substantially stronger with an optimized liquidation point.
H3: Early land + worker investment compounds when payback occurs before the horizon closes.
H4: Shop demand predicts realized profitability better than raw price.
H5: A deterministic strategy selector can beat pure RL in the current meta.
H6: Movement optimization may matter more than small commodity-selection differences.
H7: Early, middle, and late game should use different objectives.
H8: Top agents may rely on a small number of optimized presets rather than fully adaptive policies.
Final strategy
Best current architecture: Optimized deterministic trajectories + multi-strategy selector + conservative watcher + local market/weed corrections + optional learned strategy switching.
The central development principle is to make the system increasingly intelligent only where intelligence buys measurable money. First discover profitable trajectories. Then discover when they fail. Then learn when switching is worth its cost. This keeps the agent aligned with the actual Kaggriculture objective: maximizing final cash per action rather than maximizing model sophistication.
